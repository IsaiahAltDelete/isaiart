Starting points taken from the eras themselves. Move any slider afterwards — the preset is a seed, not a lock.
02 SIGNAL
The vaseline. Luma is resolved far more sharply than colour on tape, so this stays gentle — push CHROMA BLEED for the smeared look instead. Colour smeared sideways while the outlines stay put. This is the single control that reads most as "video" — VHS carried colour at a fraction of the luminance bandwidth, so reds run into their neighbours. Colour arriving late, so it sits to the right of the object it belongs to. The bright lip trailing every dark edge — aggressive analogue sharpening overshooting. Small amounts do a lot of the period feel.
03 LEVELS
The faded part. Tape never reached black, so shadows sit milky and grey — this is what stops a filter looking like a modern photo with grain on it. Negative cools the shadows blue, positive pushes them magenta — the tell-tale cast of a warm dub.
04 GLOW
Highlights blooming into what surrounds them, warm-tinted the way film and tube phosphor both bloom. Studio key lights are why every one of these clips glows.
05 NOISE
Chroma noise is the coarse blotchy kind that drifts in flat colour — a different animal from film grain, and much more of a giveaway that this is tape. Faint horizontal banding that shifts down the frame. Not scanlines — dropout and tracking, which is the texture these actually have.
06 FRAME
The ragged dark border where the picture runs past the tube's usable area. Left at zero on purpose. Broadcast video on a consumer set had no visible line structure — drawn-on scanlines are the single thing that most makes a "VHS filter" look fake. It is here if you want it. The torn, skewed strip at the very bottom of the frame where the tape head changes over. Genuinely diagnostic of VHS and almost never faked.
